# Radio Milwaukee's data platform: what it is and what it costs

A one-page summary for leadership and funders. June 2026.

## The problem we're solving

Radio Milwaukee's data lives in a dozen places: streaming reports, Google
Analytics, Facebook and Instagram, Mailchimp, the donation system, and finance
spreadsheets. Each tool shows one slice. No one can sit down and see how it all
connects, like whether a social campaign moved streaming numbers, or which
audiences are growing and which are slipping. Answering a cross-cutting
question today means pulling several exports by hand and stitching them
together in a spreadsheet.

## What we're building

One database that holds all of it, organized consistently, kept current
automatically. On top of that, an app built in Hex that each team opens to see
the numbers that matter to them: programming sees content and dayparts,
development sees audience growth and donors, underwriting sees inventory and
delivery, leadership sees revenue against budget. Built once, updated on its
own, no manual spreadsheet wrangling.

## What it costs Radio Milwaukee

The software is close to free. The one paid tool is the service that pulls data
in automatically, about $65 a month. The database and the dashboard tool run on
free tiers for now. As we roll the app out to the full staff, those will move
to paid tiers, so a realistic steady-state budget is roughly
**$3,000 to $8,000 a year** in tooling. But because of nonprofit rates, we are looking at less than $1000/year

The larger investment is the build itself, which we're doing in-house with
AI-assisted development rather than hiring it out.

## What this normally costs

For comparison, here's what an organization typically pays to get the same
thing (industry estimates, not quotes):

- **Hiring an agency to build it:** $40,000 to $150,000 up front, plus a
  $2,000 to $8,000 a month retainer to maintain it.
- **Buying the enterprise version of this stack** (the brand-name ingestion,
  warehouse, and dashboard tools): $15,000 to $70,000 a year, every year.
- **Hiring a data engineer in-house:** $120,000 to $140,000 a year in salary
  and benefits.

## Why this matters

We're building capability that organizations our size usually can't afford, and
keeping the recurring cost low enough that it doesn't compete with the mission
for dollars. The streaming side already runs end to end and proves the approach
works. The website data is now live in the warehouse. Everything after this is
repeating a proven pattern, one source at a time, until the whole organization
is working from the same set of numbers.

The money we're not spending on an agency or enterprise licensing stays where
it should: on the work.
